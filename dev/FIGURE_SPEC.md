# The prince's body and poses (spec)

## The rule (round 23): the body and every pose come from motion capture

Owner, after round 22d: "Start from those clips and build the athletic teen prince body and scrap the current skeleton and proportions
so that we don't get swayed in the wrong direction." The skeleton, the drawing's widths and every pose are generated by
`dev/tools/r23poses.py` from measured data; nothing is fitted to the earlier figures. The rounds 22b-22d sections below are superseded.

Sources:
- CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database, http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu ("The data used in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu.
  The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217."). Per subject an .asf skeleton, per trial an .amc of joint angles at
  120 fps (`dev/tools/ref/cmu/`, not in git: `index.tsv` lists the clips; fetch an .asf/.amc from
  `http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/subjects/NN/NN_MM.amc`). Parsed by `dev/tools/mocap.py` (units: `:units length 0.45`, so metres = value /
  0.45 x 0.0254; forward kinematics L = C M C^-1 per bone, M = Rz Ry Rx of its dofs).
- ANSUR II (US Army, 2012; calmcode.io's copy in `dev/tools/ref/ansur2_ergonomics.csv`), men aged 17 to 20: the lean ones (BMI under 23,
  n = 124) for the trunk's lengths and depths, all 422 for the limbs' girths and the chest (an athlete's muscle on a lean frame).

ANSUR's (round 23c: the chin 2 px nearer the joint, see below). Measured on the drawing (`egyptr23.py body`, r23measure.json):

### The head: 6.5 heads (decision)

A real 17 to 20 year old is 7.7 heads tall (ANSUR stature 1764.5 mm; head 228 mm, 0.13 of stature, Drillis and Contini 1966); Loomis
(Figure Drawing for All It's Worth, 1943) draws an average man at 7.5 heads and a youth a little less. Games with a figure of this
height keep the head larger than real so the face reads: the 1989 Prince of Persia kid, rotoscoped from Mechner's brother, is 41 rows
with a 7-row head, 5.86 heads (measured from his DOS frames, round 22d); the other rotoscoped side-view games of the time (Another
World, Flashback) draw 6.5 to 7 heads (round 22b's survey); stylised platformers go to 2 to 3. Our figure is 52 px at 1x and drawn
at 2 to 3 device pixels a game pixel on the phone, so the eye, brow, nose and jaw need about 8 px of head: **6.5 heads**, between
the real 7.7 and the kid's 5.86. Everything below the chin is real, only the head is enlarged (1.24 times real).

### The poses (MCP, MCK in the source)

Every pose is the game's angles (`skel`: lean and head from straight up, the limbs from straight down, knees and elbows as the bend
from the bone above, positive forward) measured from one clip frame, or a short average, projected onto the plane of the body's own
facing (the pelvis's forward, `mocap.facing`). The near side ('F', drawn last) is the subject's right, or his left in the fight
poses (his lead leg). What places a pose in the game is v69's and unchanged: air, hy (the hip's height in the air), sw (the
blade's angle), rot, kn, and the held hands (hold/hb: the hand goes to v69's place, `r23grips.json`, moved with the hip on the
ground). Where the new arm cannot reach v69's grip, the trunk is turned toward it (logged by r23poses.py: pull 0.41 rad, garde 0.31,
lunge 0.46, parry 0.07, lowStrike 0.10). The low leaps keep their legs and are pitched forward until the head is no higher than v69's.

the drink's 1.3 s and its beats, the swing's physics are unchanged; the clips only give each frame's shape.

### How to add or change a move

1. Find a clip in `dev/tools/ref/cmu/index.tsv`, fetch its .amc (and the subject's .asf) into `dev/tools/ref/cmu/`.
2. Look at it: `python3 mocap.py sheet CLIP a b step out.png` (a contact sheet in the side view) and `mocap.py video CLIP frame`.
3. Add or change its line in `SEL` (or a cycle in `build()`) of `r23poses.py`; run it: it rewrites the block between `// <R23-MOCAP>`
   and `// </R23-MOCAP>` in the source and prints each pose's source and any grip correction.
4. Check: `figscan.py` (held hands, crown, soles), `egyptr23.py moves` (mocap | new | v69), lowceil, stairscan, swingtest.

### Checks (round 23)

`figscan.py` against v69: held hands within 0.35 px (0.75 for the lunges), on the lip while pulling up and on the floor while pressing
up, the crawl's palms on the floor, no crown higher than v69's standing crown in any pose (and no higher than v69's own in the crawl,
the low leaps and the slide), the sole on the floor where v69's was, the swing's hands on the bar, the drink's hand at the mouth.

## Muscle bulk (round 23b): one multiplier, MUS

Owner: "Yeah needs to be bulkier. Add a bulk multiplier, but in the sense of more muscle. Give me three variants of him with the current size
then more and more bulky". The owner picked 1.28 in round 23c; round 23b itself drew 1.0 (round 23, unchanged pixel for pixel).

The rule: `MUS` (r23poses.py) is the upper arm's girth over round 23's (ANSUR II's median). Every other measure grows at its own rate per
unit of the upper arm's (`GROW`): `factor = 1 + rate x (MUS - 1)`. Joints (elbow, wrist, knee, ankle), bones, the skeleton (R23B), every pose,
the held hands, hit boxes and timing never change. The waist barely grows (no fat), so the V-taper sharpens. Robbers keep 1.15 times the
prince's breadth on top of the level.

Round 23c: the owner picked 1.28 ("1.28 is fine"); the game drew it until round 24 (now 1.65, below) with no override (`MUS` in r23poses.py; R23D is the 1.28 table, R23DV keeps the others).

| Measure | Rate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| upper arm (biceps) | 1.00 | the multiplier itself |
| deltoid (profile) | 1.20 | ANSUR deltoid soft tissue (bideltoid less biacromial) grows faster than the arm |
| above the elbow (brachialis, triceps) | 0.85 | estimate between biceps and forearm |
| forearm | 0.55 | ANSUR 0.66 (17-20) and 0.48 (17-24, frame out) |
| chest depth | 0.80 | ANSUR 0.91 / 0.72 |

| trapezius slope | 0.90 | estimate (neck plus trapezius, Reeves) |
| neck, its base | 0.55, 0.50 | ANSUR 0.47 / 0.60 |
| thigh, mid-thigh | 0.70 | ANSUR 0.86 / 0.60 |
| above the knee (vastus) | 0.60 | ANSUR lower thigh 0.69 / 0.61 |
| calf | 0.55 | ANSUR 0.64 / 0.48; laid behind the shank (`RD.cb`), the shin straight |
| lower calf (soleus) | 0.45 | estimate |
| seat depth (glutes) | 0.60 | ANSUR 0.83 / 0.48; sprinters' glutes |
| waist depth | 0.12 | held: the data's waist grows with the heaviest men's fat |

The trunk's nine levels (`TRUNK_G`) take the measure at that level and lay its growth in front or behind: glutes 80-85% behind, the waist
half and half, the lats 70% behind, the chest 45-55% in front, the trapezius 65% behind and up the slope from C7.

The levels (`LEVELS`, carried in the source as `R23MUS`/`R23DV`; `window.__MUS` before load or `__HK.setMus(m)` draws one):

| MUS | Basis |
|---|---|
| 1.0 | round 23: ANSUR II medians, men 17-20 |
| 1.10 | athletic: ANSUR II ~90th percentile of muscle among the lean, the frame taken out |
| 1.18 | strong: ANSUR II top 3-5% of the lean; a sprinter or gymnast (Heath-Carter mesomorphy ~6) |
| 1.28 | natural near-limit: between McCallum's and Reeves's ratios (arm 1.21-1.31), fat-free mass index ~24-25 (Kouri et al. 1995) |

ANSUR II analysis: men 17-20 with waist/chest under the median (n=211), ranked by girths for stature (biceps, forearm, calf, thigh, chest,
shoulder); 97th / 50th percentile group: biceps 1.17, chest depth 1.16, thigh 1.15, buttock depth 1.14, forearm 1.12, calf 1.11, neck 1.08,
waist depth 1.13 (BMI 30: fat; held here). A second cut (17-24, lean by waist/stature, n=679) agrees and shows the frame grows too (wrist
+5.5%), so half of that comes off the limbs.

Drawing (Proko on the deltoid and calf; small-sprite practice: silhouette and value, not lines): muscle reads in the outline. Above 1.0 two
faint strokes in the part's own shade grow with the level (`RD.mh`, 0 at 1.0, 0.6 at 1.28): the deltoid's V into the upper arm at its
insertion (0.47 down the humerus) and the pectoral's short lower border (fading as the arms rise). Sources and pictures: egypt_themes.html,
round 23b (`egyptr23b.py`). Check a level with figscan and lowceil with `window.__MUS` set.
